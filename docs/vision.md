# Vision

## The problem

Most habit apps are built for people who already have their life in order. They
remind you, they show you a nice chart, and they count on your motivation and
discipline to do the rest. But the people who download a habit app are usually
the ones who struggle with exactly that.

I've tried to build habits many times (skincare, working out, studying) and
almost none of them stuck. The ones that did stick had something in common: I
had no other choice. I never skipped a shower before school, because going to
school meant I had to shower. Motivation and discipline never worked for me.
Being in a situation where the habit was the only logical option did.

## The idea

Déclic ties your habits to something you already do every day without fail:
opening certain apps on your phone.

You choose which apps get blocked. When it's time, they lock, and the only way to
get them back is to go do the habit. For habits tied to a place, an NFC tag makes
this physical: you put a tag next to your bathroom mirror, and you have to get up
and scan it. Once you're standing there, doing the routine is the easy part.

The tag doesn't prove you did the habit. It puts you where the habit happens,
and that turns out to be enough.

It's also a real habit tracker, with history, streaks and stats, because the
blocking alone isn't enough. You want to see the progress.

## Who it's for

People who struggle to keep habits, and for whom motivation and discipline
haven't worked. That includes people with ADHD.

It's built for people whose life isn't regular yet, with no fixed wake-up or
bed time. The timing doesn't assume a perfect schedule. For example, apps can
lock again a few hours after your last unlock, instead of at fixed times.

It should still work for people who already have good habits, and people who
get better should be able to stay and keep tracking their routines.

## What makes it different

A habit tracker gives recommendations. An app blocker takes things away. Déclic
does both, and they work better together: the blocker makes the habit the only
way forward, and the tracker shows you it's working.

## How it should feel

Calm, but not a meditation app. Like a clean room: the bed is made, everything
is in its place.

Missing a day is normal. No streak shaming. The app should help people build
habits that are realistic for them, and make it clear that a broken streak
isn't the end of the world.

## Not in v1

- iOS (Android first, iOS later)
- Accounts, login or a backend. Everything stays on the phone for now, but the
  design shouldn't rule out a backend later.
- Social features, like a friend getting notified when you skip. Maybe v2.
- Verifying that the habit was actually done, beyond being where the tag is
- Ads or any kind of monetization
- Heavy gamification. Streaks are in, levels maybe later, no game vibe.
- Blocking the whole phone. The user picks the apps.

## What success looks like for v1

Déclic runs on my phone, end to end, and I use it every day for one to two
weeks instead of my current MacroDroid setup.
